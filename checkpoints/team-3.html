02 · The thing nobody in the channel has said out loud

You have written the same film three times

  THE LAST DAY      a dying man crosses into a DREAM WORLD he has never seen
  THE MAPMAKER      a blind woman crosses into a CITY she has never seen
  THE PINK DOOR     a boy crosses through a DOOR into a WORLD he has never seen
                                    │
                    ────────────────┴────────────────
                    someone crosses a threshold into
                     a world they cannot see — and
                      has to trust what's on the
                              other side

In plain English: three different stories, one obsession. That's not a coincidence — it's the team's actual taste.

Finding

The threshold is the team's real idea. The story around it is still open.

It shows up in the language too. On Aug 10, on The Last Day, Danney wrote "would be cool to have different doors lead to different world of art theme." Two days later Dan proposed the same structure on a completely different concept — jungle, ocean and city each in their own art style. The idea migrated between stories because it was never really about either story.

Which means the argument in this channel isn't "which concept?" It's "which story best carries the thing we already agree on?" That is a much easier question, and it's the one the vote should ask.

Built from what you've each actually said, not from roles. This is the part that carries to the next film.

Dan Ramm

The developer — interrogates, then builds

Has not pitched a concept of his own. Instead he takes someone else's and asks the structural question nobody asked: what's the conflict, what's the resolution, why is she a mapmaker if she's blind. Then he hands back beats. His Pink Door notes solved the ending problem better than the review did.

References he brings: What Dreams May Come · Bioshock Infinite · Inception · Annihilation · The Last of Us · I Am Legend · Pluribus · The Leftovers · Oblivion

Chillucci

The architect — writes it whole or not at all

The only person who has answered every question about their own concept before being asked twice. The Mapmaker has a stated conflict, a stated climax, and — rarest of all — a climax that isn't the plot resolving: her fingers find the carved detail from the letter, and that, not finding her son, is the emotional peak.

Reaches for: conceptual payoffs over plot payoffs, and internal consistency. Also the room's IP conscience — he's the one who spotted Tarzan.

Hannahh

Reaches for: wonder, motion, and a strong single image. Her instinct produced the one thing on the slate nobody else has — a door onto open ocean.

04 · Where you disagree without having noticed

Open, and worth settling today

Dan and Hannah effectively endorsed the Mapmaker — in a voice call the others missed

Surface it Dan, Aug 12: "Hannah and i talked but didn't want to riff really in anything apart from Chillucci's idea since it seems thorough and cool." That's two of five leaning one way, said once, in passing, and never carried into the channel as a position. It should be on the ballot.

Two concepts want opposite production shapes

Decide first The Mapmaker is small by design — one city, one character, resolved by touch. The Last Day is large by design — many worlds, many art styles. Those aren't two flavours of the same plan; they're opposite bets on where 22 days should go.

"Different art styles per world" is unresolved craft, not a settled look

Test it It's the team's favourite idea and it has never been challenged. The risk is craft-on-display, which all three judges reject. The safer version — one style, worlds separated by palette and light — has not been argued against yet. Argue it.

Tarzan

Design around it Chillucci flagged it; Hannah hasn't seen the film. Not fatal — gate 07 bars third-party IP, not premises — but if Pink Door wins, the monkey and boy designs need an explicit off-model reference sheet before anything is generated.

06 · Suggested moves

Three things to look at — not decisions

1. Vote today, and put all three on the ballot

Why It's already a day late, the Room tab exists for it, and two people have a stated lean that never made it into the channel. A vote taken now costs nothing; a concept chosen by drift costs three weeks.

2. Ask the easier question instead of "which concept?"

Why All three are the same idea — someone crossing into a world they can't see. Ask "which story carries that best in three minutes, with 22 days?" That question has a defensible answer; "which do you like?" doesn't.

3. Whatever wins, steal from the other two

Why The Mapmaker's climax structure — proof of trust lands before the plot resolves — is the strongest single beat anyone has written, and it is portable. Pink Door's door-onto-ocean is the strongest image. The Last Day's art-world doors are the strongest hook. None of those are locked to their concept.
