THE PINK DOOR
— read against the jury

A strong premise, a real reveal, and a threshold at the centre of it — which happens to be the exact thing the newest judge has built a thirty-year career on. Attached to the largest production surface anyone has proposed, and a protagonist who currently does not change. Here is what that means with twenty-two days on the clock.

Prepared 2026-08-12 Deadline Sep 3, 11:59pm PT Jury Catmull · Papamichael · Anderson Verdict Keep — aim it at Anderson

The short answer

Yes, it is a good concept — and it is not yet a story. What you have is an excellent premise (a door that shouldn't exist, opening onto the wrong world) and one genuinely good turn (the monkeys aren't stealing, they're building). Both of those are hard to invent and you have them.

The war room's own doctrine tells you what to do with that. It says a third juror is a third way to be forgiven, not a third bar to clear — that a film excellent at one thing and competent elsewhere beats a film that is uniformly good, because it gives one person in the room something to fight for. So stop trying to make Pink Door please all three. Aim it at Anderson and make the other two unable to object.

That is a much better position than it sounds, because of a detail on his judge card most people will skim past. Anderson's stated signature — the thing he says runs through every film he has made — is the doorway. "Pushing through a proscenium arch, into a scene." Your entire concept is a boy stepping through a threshold into a world that shouldn't be on the other side of it. Nobody else on the slate is handing him that.

What still has to be fixed is the thing Catmull grades hardest: the boy ends the film exactly where he started, having lost nothing. Curious, then scared, then helpful, then home. That is the shape Catmull has spent forty years rejecting — and it costs you one scene to fix, not a rewrite. And the second problem is days, not taste: jungle + open ocean + underwater + shoreline + streets + rooftops + markets + traffic is the biggest build on the slate by a wide margin, in twenty-two days.

01 · The single best argument for this concept

Anderson's signature is the doorway. You built a film about a door.

This is the part of the war room worth re-reading before Team 3 meets, because it is the thing that makes Pink Door a contender rather than a nice idea.

Paul W. S. Anderson joined the jury on Aug 6, and his card lists three quotes. Two are about speed and locations. The third is the one that matters here — it is filed under "on doorways, and why they run through all his films":

"Pushing through a proscenium arch, into a scene." Paul W. S. Anderson

A boy in a jungle pushes through a standing pink door and falls into the middle of an ocean. That is the proscenium arch, done literally, and it is the image the whole concept is organised around. His card also asks for doorways, thresholds, descents — space that reveals itself as you move through it. You have a threshold in act one, a descent into water immediately after, and a second threshold built by hand in act four.

No other concept on the slate offers him this, and the war room's own strategy note explains why that is worth more than being liked by everybody: "Jurors argue for what they love. A film that is excellent at one thing and competent elsewhere beats a film that is uniformly good — because it gives one person in that room something to fight for."

Coordinate this

Concept 07 — BREACH — is already fishing in the same water

BREACH was written after Anderson was named, specifically because nothing else on the slate was built for him, and the war room rates it 3/3/3. Its pitch is a stairwell — "the most readable space in cinema" — with a threat and a rule the audience can learn.

The assignment rule is one lane per team, no two teams take the same shape, and near-duplicate entries can be collapsed into one. Pink Door and BREACH are not the same film, but they are courting the same juror through the same door-and-geography grammar. Someone has to say out loud which team owns the Anderson lane before both spend three weeks on it.

One more thing from the channel, and treat it as the brief: zebruin pointed out that Anderson's best moment in Resident Evil was a blast door he couldn't afford — played as a shadow and a spotlight on cardboard, and more emotional than the door would have been. Anderson does not reward an expensive-looking chase. He rewards an invented one. Which is fortunate, because the invented one is the only version you can finish by Sep 3.

02 · The panel

How each of the three will read this film

A film needs two of these three to place. Right now Pink Door has one strong yes, one conditional, and one likely no.

EC
Edwin Catmull

Co-founder of Pixar · ex-President, Pixar & Disney Animation · 5× Oscar · Turing Award

Conditional — fixable in one scene

He invented the category your monkeys belong to. Luxo Jr., 1986: a non-human thing given a want. Wordless, child-legible, found-family — this is his native language and half the concept sits comfortably inside it.

Then it hands him the one shape he reliably rejects. His published test is a character who wants something and changes, and an ending that costs the character something. Your boy wants nothing at the start, loses nothing at the end, and spends act three helping someone else's plan. The chase, meanwhile, escalates spectacle without escalating feeling — his named kill, verbatim.

Lands
  • Wordless emotional logic a child can follow
  • Non-human characters with a genuine want
  • Stylization — animation is his first language, not a lesser form
  • The reveal: theft re-read as devotion
Dies
  • A protagonist who ends where he began
  • An ending that costs nobody anything
  • Spectacle escalating with no emotional escalation
  • World-building with nobody to care about
PP
Phedon Papamichael, ASC

Cinematographer · 2× Oscar & BAFTA nominee · Nebraska, Ford v Ferrari, Walk the Line

The hard no — and the reason to cut scope

He shot a whole feature in black and white about a man walking to Nebraska. His stated preference is one location used honestly, light with a source you can point at, and coverage that actually cuts. He reads a film for eyeline, screen direction, and whether the space holds together between shots.

Pink Door currently offers him seven-plus environments, water, crowds, vehicles and a variable number of near-identical animals — every one of which is a place the geometry can change between cuts. He explicitly rejected panning wide shots on Ford v Ferrari as the grammar of car commercials. A rooftop-and-market montage is the exact thing he was rejecting.

Lands
  • The jungle: one location, one light source, honest
  • A held face — the boy at the open door, before he steps
  • Texture, grain, the look of a real lens
Dies
  • Geometry that changes between cuts
  • Coverage with no master — a chase that never establishes its space
  • Mixed colour temperature with no justification
  • The unmotivated floating push-in
PA
Paul W. S. Anderson

Director · the Resident Evil franchise · Mortal Kombat · Event Horizon · Alien vs. Predator · joined the jury Aug 6

Your yes — build the film for him

Not Paul Thomas Anderson — a completely different filmmaker, and confusing them in a pitch will cost you. This is the genre craftsman: kinetic, physical, unapologetic. Thirty years of films where something is hunting someone through a space, and unusually rigorous about making that space readable.

The doorway quote is the strongest single argument this concept has. His other two are near-instructions for your second act: "No slow motion — the action is bone-crunchingly fast" and "big, gritty, real locations." A monkey chase through a market is his register exactly — provided you never lose track of where anyone is. He also insists his own designers play the games he adapts, because he wants the DNA, not the logo. So don't make a film that looks like his. Make one built the way his are.

Lands
  • Doorways, thresholds, descents — space that reveals itself as you move through it
  • Readable geography — the audience always knows where the threat and the exit are
  • Genre played straight — no apology, no irony
  • Momentum: a film going somewhere at speed
  • Production design that builds a world, not a backdrop
Dies
  • A mood piece with no engine
  • Action you cannot follow — cut so fast or close the space dissolves
  • Slow motion used to manufacture weight
  • A concept that stays a concept and never becomes a scene
  • Pastiche of his own films — the DNA, not the costume

04 · The balance sheet

Pros and cons, ranked by how much they matter

What is genuinely strong

  1. One repeatable iconThe pink door is a poster, a packshot, a thumbnail and a title card in a single object. Nothing else on the slate has that. It is also why this is our best shot at Audience Choice — a separate $100,000 lane the quiet films cannot win.
  2. It can be wordlessNo lipsync risk, no language barrier for a global jury, and no dependency on a good voice performance. That removes the biggest technical failure mode in AI shorts and the biggest craft failure mode at once.
  3. The reveal is real"They're not stealing, they're building" retroactively re-reads every previous shot. That is a genuine turn, not a twist ending bolted on — and it is a hard thing to invent.
  4. Non-human characters with a wantThe monkeys are the most Catmull-shaped element in the whole concept, and right now they're supporting cast.
  5. Anderson has no other advocate on the slateEvery other concept we have is a two-hander in one room. Pink Door is the only one that speaks to a third of the jury.
  6. The team wants to make itNot a soft point. Catmull's most-quoted line is that a brilliant team will fix a mediocre idea, and a mediocre team will ruin a good one. Conviction is a real input.

What will lose you the festival

  1. Nobody pays anythingThe boy leaves home, sees the world, comes home. Nothing is risked, nothing is lost, nothing is learned that costs. This is Catmull's primary rejection criterion and it is currently sitting in the middle of the film.
  2. The scope is the largest on the slateJungle, ocean surface, underwater, shoreline, city street, rooftops, market, traffic, build site. Generated from scratch, on-platform only, in twenty-two days. Water and crowds are the two most expensive things in AI video.
  3. "The monkeys" is an unbounded castA troop means an unknowable number of near-identical characters that must stay consistent across ~45 shots. This is the worst case our element registry can be handed. Three named monkeys is a production; a troop is a gamble.
  4. The protagonist is passiveHe is curious, then he runs, then he hides, then he watches, then he helps. He never makes a decision that costs him. Meanwhile the monkeys drive the entire third act.
  5. The stated theme isn't in the plot"Never judge a book by its cover" — nothing in the story is about appearances deceiving. That mismatch isn't a labelling error; it's the signal that the theme hasn't been found yet.
  6. The chase is the AI-trailer trap wearing a costumeRooftops, markets, traffic, montage. Without a master shot and a fixed landmark it becomes forty disconnected beauty shots — the exact pile the research says eliminates itself at shortlist.
  7. Derivative surface areaMonsters Inc doors, Slumberland, Jumanji, Aladdin's Abu. No IP is being used, so this is not an eligibility problem — it is a scoring problem on Originality, and it is entirely a design-language decision.

05 · The fix

Three changes and this becomes the strongest entry we have

None of these touch the premise, the door, the reveal, or the ending image. All three can be decided in one meeting.

1

Give the ending a cost

The door needs one last piece and there is nothing left to take. The boy gives up the one thing he carried out of the jungle — or one monkey stays on the city side, holding the frame open, and does not come through. Someone loses something. That is the entire fix.

Then plant its opposite at the top: give the boy a want before the door. He is outgrowing the jungle. He is the only one of his kind in it. Now the city tempts him, going home becomes a choice instead of a default, and the climax stops being "the door is finished" and becomes "he decides to walk through it."

Fixes: Catmull · Directing & Storytelling · the missing theme

2

Cast three monkeys, not a troop

Three named characters with distinct silhouettes — one blessed reference each in the element registry, cap of four elements in frame. Crowd density only in wide shots you generate once and never have to match. Three characters can also carry a personality each, which a troop never can, and it gives you a specific monkey to lose in Fix 1.

Fixes: consistency · Visual Craft · your credit burn

3

Keep the ocean. Shrink the city to three places.

The door opening onto open water is the most original image in the concept and the one nobody else at this festival will have — protect it. Then hold the city to one street, one high place, one build site, established with a real master shot and one landmark visible from every angle so the geography always reads.

That is not a compromise, it is the Anderson brief: invention under constraint. A chase told through reaction, sound, and a single repeated landmark will beat a montage of rooftops that never cut together — and it is the only version you can finish by Sep 3.

Fixes: Papamichael · scope · the AI-trailer trap

The shape after the fix

        BEFORE  (round trip — nothing changes)

  jungle ──► door ──► ocean ──► city ──► chase ──► build ──► jungle
   happy                                                      happy


        AFTER  (a want, a temptation, a price)

  jungle ──► door ──► ocean ──► city ──► chase ──► build ──► jungle
  restless            wonder    tempted   cost     choice     home
     │                                      │                   ▲

                                    he gives something up ──────┘

In plain English: same beats, same images, same ending shot — but now he leaves wanting something, the city offers it to him, and going home is a choice he pays for.

06 · Before anything else

The questions the team has to answer

Grouped by what they cost you if you get them wrong. Each carries a recommended answer — argue with it, but do not leave it blank.

Answer today

One rule could kill this concept outright, and our own summary of it is ambiguous

Eligibility gate 07 in our research reads: "no sexual content, no character depicted under 18." Read plainly and out of context, that prohibits a child protagonist — and our lead is a boy. Read in context, it is almost certainly a rule about sexualising minors.

Do not proceed on the charitable reading. Someone pulls the actual festival terms and quotes the clause verbatim in the channel before a single credit is spent on a character design. If it is genuinely a blanket ban, this concept needs an adult or non-human lead — a change you want to make on day one, not on day fourteen.

Eligibility — these end the entry, not just weaken it

No judge sees the film until all nine gates clear.

1. Does the age clause prohibit a child protagonist, or only sexualisation of minors?

Recommend Quote the clause verbatim before designing the lead. If ambiguous, age him to eighteen-plus — it costs the concept almost nothing and removes the only existential risk on the page.

2. Who posts publicly, from which public account, and on what date?

Recommend Assign a name now. Higgsfield names the missing social post as the single most common reason a strong entry is ruled non-eligible — it must be a public account, viewable logged-out, live through the end of the festival.

3. Is anyone on Team 3's roster also on another team's roster?

Recommend Audit all five rosters this week. One shared name caps both entries at a single prize between them — leader or not. Keep every roster disjoint.

4. Has the Reelix-to-submission-project pre-flight actually been fired?

Recommend Do it before any real generation. If Reelix generations land in the general library instead of inside the submission project, the asset audit fails. This one test decides the pipeline architecture for all five teams.

5. Is every named team member subscription-active — both at project creation and at submission?

Recommend Confirm in writing per person, and budget the renewals now across all five teams rather than discovering a lapse on the 30th.

6. Who owns the project, and is the cross-team split written down?

Recommend Name the Team Leader (project owner and payee) and sign the split before submission. Higgsfield explicitly will not mediate intra-team distribution — it is a private contract they never see.

Story — answer before a single shot is generated

Every one of these is cheap now and expensive on August 25th.

7. What does the boy lose? One sentence.

Recommend A monkey stays behind. It is the cheapest to shoot, the hardest to forget, and it makes the reveal hurt instead of charm.

8. Whose film is this — the boy's or the monkeys'?

Recommend Write the answer at the top of the script and never contradict it. If it stays the boy's, he must drive act three. If it becomes the monkeys', you inherit the Luxo Jr. lineage and Catmull's full attention — the stronger option, and the harder script.

9. What does the boy want in the jungle, before the door appears?

Recommend Something the city can offer him. Without it the door is an accident that happens to a contented boy, and there is no arc to grade.

10. Why does he choose to go back, when the city has what he wanted?

Recommend Make this the climax — not the completion of the door. The door being finished is a plot event; his decision is the film.

11. What is the theme, stated as something that happens in the plot?

Recommend Drop "never judge a book by its cover" — it isn't in the story. What is in the story is home is who follows you into the water. Write the version you can point at on screen.

12. What are the rules of the door — and does it open again?

Recommend Decide and never break it. One rule, established in act one, honoured in act four. Undefined magic is where child audiences check out and juries stop trusting you.

13. What are the four act lengths against a 3:20 target?

Recommend Budget the minutes before you write. Three minutes is a hard floor — 2:59 is not screened. Four acts in 3:20 means roughly 40s jungle / 40s ocean / 80s city / 40s home, and the chase is the part that will try to eat everyone else's time.

Craft — these decide whether you finish

Consistency is the whole game at this scope.

14. Stylized or photoreal?

Recommend Stylized. It is Catmull's first language, it sidesteps the uncanny valley that will sink half the field, it is the most distinctive thing you can hand a jury watching photoreal entry after photoreal entry — and photoreal monkeys in water in twenty-two days is a coin flip.

15. Wordless, or dialogue?

Recommend Wordless. Zero lipsync risk, no language barrier for a global jury, and it forces the sound design to carry — your strongest criterion.

16. How many named monkeys, and are their silhouettes distinguishable in one frame?

Recommend Three. One blessed reference each in the element registry, locked before any shot generation, cap of four elements in frame.

17. Where does the light come from, in each environment?

Recommend Write one sentence per environment and put it in the DNA. It is the first thing Papamichael reads, and unjustified mixed colour temperature is on his named kill list.

18. What is the master shot of the city sequence, and what landmark is visible from every angle?

Recommend Generate the master first and cut everything against it. "Coverage with no master" is the exact failure that turns a chase into forty disconnected beauty shots.

19. What is the drop-dead date for locked character refs and the style sheet?

Recommend Aug 15. Nothing generated before the sheet is locked survives, and every day of slippage comes straight out of the edit at the end.

20. When does Team 3 screen its first assembly to the other teams?

Recommend Put a Braintrust date on the Schedule tab now — around Aug 22. Candid notes, no authority: the room advises, the director decides. It is Catmull's own method and he would recognise it.
